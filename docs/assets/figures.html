Figure 1 · The failure chain

A full disk becomes a frozen machine

Nothing here is a hardware fault. Each step is ordinary behaviour; the damage is in the sequence — and it only starts once free space runs out.

1
The disk passes ~90% full
On this machine: 36.6 GB free of 494 GB.
2
No headroom left for anything
Snapshots, swap growth, working space — all competing for 36.6 GB. vm.swapusage reads total = 0.00M, which looks healthy in isolation and tells you almost nothing on its own.
3
Memory pressure loses its relief valve
23 GB of 24 GB in use, and nowhere to page out to.
4
The page cache evicts executables and mapped files
They are needed again immediately, so they are read straight back from the SSD.
5
Sustained 18 MB/s of re-reads — pagein thrash
1,043,671 pageins against 6,233 pageouts in a 16-minute uptime. Bursts hit far higher; the grind is what accumulates.
6
The kernel stalls on I/O — watchdog timeouts and app kills
Which is where you notice, and where you start blaming the drive.

Steps 1 and 3 are measured; the arrow between them is inference. 36.6 GB is ample for a swapfile, and macOS never attempted one — the compressor was coping. What is certain is that the disk was 93% full, RAM was saturated, and the drive was serving re-reads it should never have been asked for.

Figure 2 · What SMART actually said

Reads ran at nearly three times writes

Every health field on the drive was perfect. The lifetime counters were not — and the ratio between them is the diagnosis.

Data read
39.5 TB · 90 GB/hour
Data written
13.8 TB

Across 438 powered-on hours. Heavy writing means a busy machine. Heavy reading at three times your write volume means the same data being read over and over — the signature of pagein thrash, not of a dying disk.

~/.ollama is five times larger than ~/.pub-cache and roughly two thousand times cheaper to back up. Big sequential blobs stream; tiny files each pay the latency.

The cause was the same full disk: macOS purged the local snapshot Time Machine uses as its incremental reference. It stayed in the list marked (dataless) — present, holding nothing — so every backup after it failed for want of a baseline.
